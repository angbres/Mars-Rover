# MEC 203 Mars Integrity Rover

![Mars Integrity Rover — Isometric View](images/isometric_view.png)

The Mars Integrity Rover was developed for MEC 203 as a sister-rover concept inspired by NASA's Mars 2020 Perseverance mission. The project focused on maintaining the reliability of a proven six-wheel rocker-bogie platform while developing a new sample-collection system capable of securely handling objects with irregular geometry. The final design was created as a complete CAD assembly and supported by individual part models, dimensioned drawings, fit and tolerance decisions, and a full engineering report.

The design process began by comparing different robotic gripper configurations and considering the tradeoff between mechanical simplicity and stability. An early two-point grabber concept was rejected because limited contact area could allow irregular objects to slip and could create uneven torque under load. The final concept uses a five-point gripper that increases the available contact area and distributes the load more evenly, allowing the mechanism to either secure an object at several contact points or surround it more completely during collection.

![Mars Integrity Rover — Top View](images/top_view.png)

The rover chassis acts as the structural center of the assembly and provides mounting locations for the suspension, robotic arm, solar panels, camera system, and power system. The chassis geometry was shaped around the suspension so the rocker-bogie mechanism could move through rough terrain without interfering with the body. Material was also removed from noncritical areas of the chassis and wheels to reduce unnecessary mass while preserving the geometry needed for structural support and operation.

The robotic arm uses two pivot locations to improve positioning of the five-point gripper during sample collection. The camera system was designed to rotate through a full 360 degrees and tilt downward to inspect both the surrounding terrain and the rover itself. At the rear of the assembly, the MMRTG power system is positioned at an angle and surrounded by protective shields intended to increase ground clearance and reduce exposure to debris.

![Mars Integrity Rover — Rear Side View](images/rear_side_view.png)

The design also considered how the assembly would function mechanically rather than treating the rover only as a visual CAD model. Different clearances were selected depending on whether a connection needed to rotate or remain fixed. Smaller clearances were used at precision pivots in the arm and claw, while larger clearances were incorporated into suspension and mounting connections where easier assembly and free movement were more important.

The complete project includes the final assembled rover, the individual CAD components used to build the assembly, and the engineering report documenting the concept-development process, design reasoning, product specifications, CAD views, and tolerance decisions. Together, the files show the progression from the original gripper concept to a complete rover assembly designed around mobility, sample collection, mechanical reliability, and manufacturability.

## Project Files

The repository contains the full MEC 203 project report as a PDF and a compressed archive containing the Fusion 360 CAD files for the rover assembly and its individual components. The report provides the design rationale and engineering documentation, while the CAD archive contains the source models used to construct the final assembly.
